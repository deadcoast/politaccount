Record 001 · Justin Trudeau · Two scales

Two scales

Operational Accountability. Provable Observable Corruption. One rulebook, written before any leader was scored, for the leaders of first-world democracies.

Scored from the 63 cases on the record. Conduct, never policy. Every point traces to a case, a date and a source. Accountability is counted, not graded: what landed on him, whether he fought it, what changed, while he held office. The law is not the bar for corruption: the common meaning of the word is, tested element by element.

Words count for nothing. Nothing after March 14, 2025 counts. Allegations nobody examined score nothing and are listed. An act cleared under a statute narrower than the word still scores on the facts admitted, and is flagged.

The rules

Scales v1.1.0 · scales.py

What the scales are for. A leader of a first-world democracy is measured against the standard that office claims for itself: the rule of law, independent officers and courts, a free press, an oath. Not against a dictator or a warlord. The scales are neutral in one specific way: they score conduct, never policy, and every rule below was fixed before any leader was scored.

What counts as evidence. Only the record: a court, an officer of Parliament, an auditor, a public inquiry, a recorded vote, sworn testimony, released documents, or their own words and acts. An allegation nobody examined is listed and scores nothing. Legality is not a test. When a statute's definition is narrower than the word, the admitted facts still score, and the line says the act was cleared under the Act.

How the two scales differ. Accountability counts what happened where it was owed: consequences, posture, change, each dated, in office. Cleared and upheld cases are out of it, neither for nor against. Corruption asks only what corrupt acts are proven, by whom, and how strongly. A leader can score badly on one and not the other.

Time. Nothing after the last day in office counts. Owning it counts only before the finding. Cooperating with a process the law requires is not owning it.

Operational Accountability — three things counted per owed case

A body found against the leader, the government or a minister, or they admitted the fault. Cleared, upheld, never-examined and cost-only cases are out: neither for nor against.

ConsequenceWhat landed on the person responsible, in office.

  1. 1On the leader personally: resigned over it, fined, sanctioned, repaid, or his own decision reversed under pressure.
  2. 0.5On the responsible minister or official: resigned or removed over it, sanctioned; or the specific decision reversed.
  3. 0Nothing. Words are nothing: an apology, 'accepting' a report, a review ordered, a recommendation accepted. Money paid by the treasury is nothing. Anything after leaving office is nothing.

PostureWhat he did with it before a body ruled.

  1. ownedAdmitted it or acted on it before any finding, and did not obstruct.
  2. nothingSaid nothing of substance, or accepted the finding only once it was made.
  3. foughtDenied, minimized, blamed others, appealed, litigated, withheld records, invoked cabinet confidence, prorogued, or refused an order.

ChangeWhether anything actually changed, in office.

  1. 1A law, a rule, a process or a decision changed, and the conduct did not recur.
  2. 0Nothing changed; a change promised, accepted or 'under implementation'; the same conduct recurred; or the change came after leaving office.

Provable Observable Corruption — the seven types and their elements

An act scores only if every element is on the record at documented grade or better; its grade is the lowest grade among its elements. A case may carry up to two further acts of a different type, each at half weight.

T1Self-dealing and conflictsbase 3

  1. a benefit — a gift, money, travel, a contract, a licence, a programme — to the office-holder, their family, their party, or a close associate
  2. the giver or recipient had, or sought, business with the government
  3. the office-holder did not refuse it, or did not recuse from the decision

T2Interference with an independent processbase 4

  1. the office-holder or their office intervened in, pressured, or publicly pre-judged
  2. a decision reserved to an independent actor: a prosecutor, a judge, the police, an officer of Parliament, an arm's-length appointment
  3. without lawful authority to direct that decision

T3Abuse of state power against peoplebase 4

  1. state power was used against people: detention, property, bank accounts, prosecution, surveillance
  2. a court found the legal conditions for using it were not met, or that it breached their rights

T4Public money to insiders, or without the rulesbase 3

  1. public funds, contracts, grants or appointments went
  2. to insiders, friends, party-connected people, ineligible recipients, or without the controls the rules require
  3. as found by an auditor, an officer of Parliament, a court, a recorded vote of Parliament, or admitted — beyond ordinary error

T5False statements to the publicbase 2

  1. a public statement of fact by the office-holder, their office, or a minister
  2. contradicted by a document, a finding, or their own later statement on the record
  3. about a matter they were in a position to know

T6Obstruction of scrutinybase 3

  1. an investigator, a committee, a court, an inquiry, or Parliament sought records or answers
  2. the office-holder or government withheld them, redacted beyond the order, ended the process (prorogation, a confidence vote, dissolution), or went to court against disclosure
  3. as shown by an order, a ruling, a formal admonishment, or the record

T7Breach of their own written standardbase 1.5

  1. a written standard the office-holder set, signed, or swore to
  2. was broken, per a finding or an admission
  3. with none of the types above present

Evidence grades

E4 × 1Adjudicated

A court, an officer of Parliament, an auditor, or a public inquiry found it.

E3 × 1Admitted / own record

They admitted it, or it is their own act or document on the record: a settlement paid, a lawsuit filed, an itinerary issued, a statement made.

E2 × 0.6Documented, no ruling

On the public record — sworn testimony, released documents, a recorded vote of the House — but no body with authority ruled on it.

E1 × 0Reported, never examined

Reported by major outlets, denied or unanswered, examined by nobody. Listed. Scores nothing.

Who did it

× 1The leader personally: his own act or statement, or a decision he announced and defended as his own
× 0.8His staff, or a cabinet decision carried by a minister
× 0.5A minister or MP, personally
× 0.4A department, agency or body under the government

Weight of the matter

S = 1 + [people harmed, or $10 million or more of public money] + [a body found against them], capped at 3. It multiplies both scales. Points on the corruption scale are base × evidence × who × S; the bands are expressed in units of a leader-level major act, 12 points: the gravest type, adjudicated, the leader personally, on a matter of weight 3.
